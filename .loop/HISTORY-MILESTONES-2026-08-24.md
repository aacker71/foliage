## M1 — a leaf you can send                                    ← NOW
**Released when: one recruiter-or-hiring-manager-shaped person who is not the owner reads the
export and, unaided, (a) names one concrete capability from it, (b) says what the proof labels
let them conclude, and (c) answers "would you act on this page — what's missing?"**
A courtesy reply is not a release. Confusion and wrong inference are recorded as findings, not
failures of the person.

Remaining, in order:
1. The PRIVATE-badge decision (owner): the export audit found all 106 rows badged
   "PRIVATE — Not shared by link" inside the folder being shared — confirm roster-is-publication
   and reword the badge for readers, or make export consult visibility.
2. Re-export at the current renderer and read the page as the stranger (the artifact in hand
   predates the blade-row and audience work).
3. The send path, named: a static host the owner controls (one-time GitHub Pages/Netlify push),
   or the folder handed directly — either is fine; an unnamed "URL" is not. FCRA-style
   disclosure step from `.loop/LAUNCH.md` P0 rides along.
4. Send it, with the three questions above.
Out of scope: canopy, enterprise, escalation, any new mechanism, any new gate.

**Released when: a second person explicitly consents to publish a named leaflet, sees exactly
what becomes public before it does, opens the resulting URL from outside their local session —
and a policy-denied or private leaflet is unreachable by direct URL and absent from every
aggregate, verified by trying.**
This is the consent-and-enforcement floor under any aggregation. In scope:
- the publish-policy DENY rail enforced on the canopy through one canonical outward-projection
  API (the leaf refuses what the canopy currently serves — corpse dated 2026-08-20);

- `verified` reachable without key ceremony (evaluate sigstore/gitsign before anything bespoke);
- approval bound to what is published (the review-subject digest — an old approval must never
  authorize text the reviewer never saw).
